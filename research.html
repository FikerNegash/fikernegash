Working Papers

  1. Don’t Know, Don’t Care: Experimental Evidence on Barriers to Responsive Public Services in Ethiopia with Daniel Agness, Pascaline Dupas, and Tigabu Getahun
    Abstract

    Using survey data on citizens, firms, and local government officials in Ethiopia’s decentralized bureaucracy, we document low knowledge of public priorities among local bureaucrats and a substantial mismatch between their priorities and those of the public. We rule out information frictions as the main cause: bureaucrats fail to update beliefs or adapt policy when given feedback on citizens’ and firms’ priorities, even in sectors over which they have high autonomy, and their demand for such information is very low. Bureaucrats also do not react when they know feedback from citizens and firms is being collected, which suggests low accountability. These results challenge standard assumptions in models of fiscal federalism and highlight the limits of decentralized bureaucracies in providing locally tailored services without incentives or intrinsic motivation to meet public demand.

  2. Road Networks, Passing Trips and Service Delivery in Rural India Preliminary results
    Abstract

    This paper develops a measure of incidental contact between citizens and local officials in rural India: how often people’s everyday journeys take them past their village council (gram panchayat) office, creating opportunities for encounters beyond the formal business of visiting it. Using public data on villages, roads, and council offices, it constructs this measure for 2,992 councils across 11 districts of Madhya Pradesh and examines how these opportunities for casual contact relate to demand for work under the rural jobs programme, where households must formally approach the council to request employment. Exploiting new rural roads built between 2016 and 2023, which changed how often people passed council offices without necessarily changing travel times, the paper isolates how opportunities for informal, everyday contact shape citizens’ engagement with the local state.

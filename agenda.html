Research Agenda

My research agenda is motivated by how quickly broad questions about the state – its size, infrastructure, reach, and moral philosophy – become personal and specific. This is especially true in places where local politics hinges less on elections. There, a citizen’s everyday encounters with public service providers – at a clinic, a school, or a council office – can matter enormously.

My research asks how people learn about citizenship through these encounters, how they make demands of the state, when they turn to providing for themselves instead, and what keeps the state from responding.

This work unfolds across two sets of questions:

  1. Barriers to responsive public services: why local bureaucrats do not act on what citizens want, even when told.
  2. Drivers of state-building trajectories in Africa: how built spaces, climate, and colonial legacies shaped where countries ended up.
